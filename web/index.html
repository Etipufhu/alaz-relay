01 — The problem

One fire.
Three different numbers.

Manavgat, Türkiye, 28–30 July 2021. Same fire, same three days, three satellites. VIIRS sees fires about ten times smaller than MODIS, so every time a new satellite joins, the raw hotspot count jumps, even if nothing changed on the ground.

02 — The relay

Every satellite
teaches the next one.

While two satellites fly together, we learn how the old one would have seen what the new one sees. Aqua and VIIRS both cross the equator around 13:30, so for fourteen years they have watched the same fires at almost the same moment. That overlap is our laboratory.

  1. 01

    Clean

    Drop heat that isn't fire: steel mills, power plants, gas flares, volcanoes. In Türkiye, 27% of VIIRS hotspots are static industrial sources, compared with 3% for MODIS.

  2. 02

    One unit

    Turn points into fire cell-days on a 0.1° grid. A burning cell counts once per day, no matter how many pixels each sensor lit up.

  3. 03

    Teach

    For every VIIRS fire, learn the chance MODIS would also have seen it, by fire power, size, day/night and region. Sum those chances to get a virtual MODIS that outlives the real one.

  4. 04

    Prove it

    Train on 2012–2017, predict 2018–2021 and compare with what Aqua actually saw: r = 0.99 across 3,965 country-months worldwide, bias +3%. The remaining error becomes the uncertainty band on every month.

What this tells you

    What is a “fire cell-day”?

    The map is cut into squares of 0.1° (about 10 × 10 km). A square counts as one fire cell-day when Aqua MODIS sees fire in it on one day. From 2012 on, when Aqua is replaced by VIIRS, we count what Aqua would have seen, so every year uses the same yardstick. 30 fire cell-days can be one large fire burning for a month, or 30 small fires on one day. It measures how often fire is seen, not hectares burned.

    Burning activity calendar

    How to read this
    • Each row is a year (2000 at the top), each column a month. Brighter square = more fire that month.
    • A bright vertical band is the fire season. A single bright square is an unusual month. A brighter row is an unusual year.
    • Click any square: its fires appear on the map, and the summary and season chart switch to that month.
    • vs. normal: red = more fire than usual for that month, blue = less, white outline = record. Raw hotspots: what other fire maps show. Everything jumps in 2012 when VIIRS joins; that is the satellite, not the fire.

    Fire season: this year vs. a normal year

    How to read this
    • Grey line: a normal year (median of 2003–2022). Grey band: the usual range (middle half of years).

    Satellites changed here. Did the fire?

    How to read this
    • Grey dashed line: raw hotspot counts per year, all satellites added up, as most fire maps do.
    • Orange line: ALAZ Relay, the same area in one consistent unit (shaded = 90% uncertainty).
    • If the grey line jumps in 2012 or 2018 while the orange line does not, the jump came from a new satellite, not from more fire. The vertical axis is logarithmic, so equal steps mean equal ratios.

    04 — What the record reveals

    Fire maps say Earth burns six times more.
    It burns about a tenth less.

    Add up every hotspot the way most fire maps do and global fire seems to explode in 2012, then again in 2018. Those are the years Suomi NPP and NOAA-20 joined. In one consistent unit the trend reverses. Aqua MODIS on its own, never harmonized, agrees.

    It finds the disasters on its own.

    We gave the detector no news, only the record. For every country and calendar month since 2012 it asks one question: is this month above every other year since 2001, even at the low end of its uncertainty? 45 months pass that test. Here are some, with the events they turned out to be. Click one to open its calendar.

    Why you draw areas

    At country level, Türkiye's August 2021 ranks only 17th of 25 Augusts, because crop-stubble burning across the southeast hides it. Draw the area around Manavgat and July–August 2021 becomes the record by a wide margin. A national total can hide the fire that matters, which is why the explorer works on any area you draw.

    Known limits

    • The unit is fire cell-days, not burned area. It measures how often fire is seen, not how much land burned.
    • From 2022 Aqua's orbit drifts later into the afternoon; we keep it as a check, not a reference.
    • NOAA-20 had only two training years (2018–19); even so its held-out error matches Suomi NPP's (see table).
    • NOAA-21 and the 2026 near-real-time stream are next in the relay.
